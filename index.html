Markets are a game. We let AI agents play it.

Every participant (a whale, a market maker, a 20x-leverage trader, a lending vault) will be played by an LLM-driven agent that stands in for one real participant, with its real positions, leverage and limits. The agents trade, hedge, panic and get liquidated against each other, under the venue's real margin and liquidation rules. Prices and crashes emerge from their game instead of being assumed.

Stage 1: Web3, where the game is visible.

On-chain exchanges publish every account's positions, leverage and liquidation price. We reverse-engineer the players: we reconstruct each account's state, learn how it actually behaves from its on-chain history, and check simulated agents against what really happened. Web3 is our wind tunnel. Near-term product: neutral, cross-venue risk infrastructure for venues, lenders and stablecoin issuers.

Stage 2: all of finance, where the game is hidden.

On-chain markets are still a small slice of global finance. In traditional markets positions are private, but the rules of the game are similar, and the method tested in Stage 1 carries over. Participants are inferred from public filings, mandates and aggregate market data, then calibrated the same way.

Every important financial decision should be simulated before the market sees it.
